# Reciprocal mutual deepening

## Purpose / when to use

Use when an audience-building relationship already shows **mutual interest** — both sides engage, reply, or support each other's content — and the next step should deepen trust without forcing urgency.

## Evidence to verify

Before recommending anything, confirm from current tool results:

- Recent interactions are **two-way** (they replied, shared, mentioned, or engaged back), not only your outbound activity.
- The person's interests, role, or topics align with your organization's audience goals.
- Prior exchanges were positive or neutral; no open complaint or boundary signal.
- You have enough context to reference something specific (a post, question, or shared topic).

Distinguish **genuine momentum** from vanity signals: a single like or follow with no reply history is not mutual deepening yet.

## Decision sequence

1. Name what is already mutual (a shared topic, exchange, or recurring engagement).
2. Choose a **small next step** that invites dialogue or collaboration — not a pitch.
3. Prefer public replies when the thread is public; move to a direct message only if context, platform norms, and prior rapport support it.
4. Offer one clear, low-friction action (question, resource, or invite) rather than a menu of asks.
5. Propose draft wording only; any send requires the normal confirmation flow.

## Adaptable phrasing patterns

- "Your point about [topic] resonated — we have been exploring [related angle]. What would you add?"
- "Appreciate you engaging on [post/thread]. Curious how you approach [specific detail they raised]."
- "We have both been talking about [theme] lately — happy to swap notes if useful."

Keep tone warm and specific; avoid manufactured scarcity or "we have to connect ASAP" language.

## Avoid / stop conditions

- Do not treat one-sided outreach as reciprocal; do not imply a relationship is closer than evidence shows.
- Do not stack multiple asks in one message.
- Do not use guilt, flattery without substance, or quid-pro-quo framing ("you owe us engagement").
- Stop if they have gone quiet after repeated outreach, declined contact, or shown poor fit — see `when-not-to-engage` and `over-invested-one-sided-cooling`.
- Do not authorize sends, ignores, or follower changes without confirmation.

## Ground in tool results

Relationship scores, interaction history, and profile data from tools outrank this playbook. If fresh data contradicts mutual-interest assumptions, revise or defer the recommendation.
