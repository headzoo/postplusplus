# Over-invested one-sided cooling

## Purpose / when to use

Use when **your side has invested more** than the other — frequent outreach, long messages, or repeated initiatives with little reciprocity — and the healthy move is to **reduce cadence and match effort** rather than escalate or confront.

## Evidence to verify

From current tool results, confirm:

- Outbound volume or initiative from your side clearly exceeds theirs over a meaningful window.
- Replies are rare, shallow, or one-directional despite multiple attempts.
- This is not a temporary busy period for an otherwise mutual relationship (check longer history).
- The person is not in an active **support** case where asymmetric effort may be appropriate (helping a customer through an issue).

## Decision sequence

1. **Pause or lengthen spacing** before the next touch — see `cadence-and-timing`.
2. Shift from initiative-heavy to **response-based** engagement: reply when they engage; avoid chasing.
3. Shorten future messages; one clear point beats long check-ins.
4. Do not confront them about "not replying" or imply punishment.
5. Leave the door open with occasional, high-value contact only when genuine news or relevance appears.
6. Recommend drafts only; no sends or relationship changes without confirmation.

## Adaptable phrasing patterns

When you do reply, keep it proportional:

- "Thanks — [brief answer]." (no extra ask)
- "[Short value-add related to their last message only]."

When recommending reduced outreach to the user, frame it as matching effort, not rejection:

- "They have not reciprocated recent outreach; suggest waiting for their next move or one light touch in [timeframe per cadence guidance]."

## Avoid / stop conditions

- No guilt, sarcasm, or "I guess you're not interested" messages.
- No doubling down with more channels or frequency to "break through."
- No treating low reciprocity as a lead to pressure harder — see `lead-follow-up-without-pressure`.
- Exception: **customer support** may warrant sustained effort even when one-sided; defer to support strategy and `support-complaint-replies`.
- Do not authorize unfollows, ignores, or blocks without confirmation.

## Ground in tool results

Interaction ratios and recency from tools define one-sidedness. A single unanswered message is not enough to cool off; use patterns, not one-off silence.
