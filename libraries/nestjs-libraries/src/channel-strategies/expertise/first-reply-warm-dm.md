# First reply warm DM

## Purpose / when to use

Use when someone has just engaged publicly (first reply, comment, or mention) and a **welcome, relevant** follow-up could start a healthy conversation — usually via public reply first.

## Evidence to verify

From current tool results, confirm:

- This is an early or first meaningful exchange, not a long-running thread with a different goal.
- Their message has enough substance to respond to (question, opinion, or specific reference).
- Public reply is appropriate for the channel and visibility of the original post.
- Direct messages are **available and appropriate** only if platform rules, their settings, and context allow — never assume DMs are open or welcome.

## Decision sequence

1. **Reply publicly first** when the conversation belongs in the thread — thank, answer, or extend their point.
2. Mirror their tone: brief for brief comments, more depth only when they invited it.
3. If a DM might help (sensitive detail, longer exchange), **signal the transition** in public or ask permission rather than cold-DMing.
4. Offer one helpful next step: answer a question, point to a resource, or invite a follow-up question.
5. Present wording as a draft; sending requires confirmation.

## Adaptable phrasing patterns

**Public first reply**

- "Thanks for jumping in — [direct answer or acknowledgment]."
- "Good question on [topic]. Short version: [helpful answer]. Happy to go deeper if useful."

**Warm DM transition (only when appropriate)**

- "Happy to share more detail — would a DM work better for [specific reason]?"
- "Sent you a note with [resource/next step] — let me know if questions come up."

Never open a DM with a pitch or fake familiarity.

## Avoid / stop conditions

- Do not DM without context, consent, or a clear reason tied to their message.
- Do not copy-paste generic welcome templates that ignore what they said.
- Do not imply you already have a relationship if this is the first exchange.
- Do not move complaints, disputes, or sales pitches into DMs to hide them from public accountability.
- Stop if they are a bot, spam, hostile, or poor fit — see `when-not-to-engage`.

## Ground in tool results

Verify the interaction is truly first/recent, the channel supports your chosen format, and organization boundaries allow this outreach. Tool data beats assumptions about DM availability or prior history.
