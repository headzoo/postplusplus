# Lead follow-up without pressure

## Purpose / when to use

Use when following up with a **prospective customer** or qualified lead where the goal is to stay helpful and trustworthy — not to close by repetition or pressure.

## Evidence to verify

From current tool results, confirm:

- There is a legitimate reason to follow up (they asked, opted in, attended, downloaded, or engaged on a relevant topic).
- You can cite **specific** prior context — not generic "just circling back."
- Fit still looks reasonable; no signals they are uninterested, wrong segment, or upset.
- Enough time has passed since the last touch that another message is considerate — see `cadence-and-timing`.

## Decision sequence

1. Lead with **relevance**: tie the message to their stated interest, question, or action.
2. Add **value** in the follow-up (answer, resource, clarification) before any ask.
3. Make the next step optional and clear — one CTA, easy to ignore without awkwardness.
4. If they have not replied to prior outreach, consider whether silence is an answer before nudging again.
5. Draft only; sends need confirmation.

## Adaptable phrasing patterns

- "You asked about [topic] — here is [specific answer/resource] in case it saves you time."
- "Following up on [event/conversation] — one thing teams often miss is [insight]. No rush if timing is off."
- "If [goal] is still on your radar, happy to [low-friction offer]. If not, no need to reply."

Avoid "checking in," "touching base," or "just wanted to bump this" without new information.

## Avoid / stop conditions

- No fake familiarity ("great chatting yesterday") unless tools show that exchange.
- No premature hard pitch on first or second touch.
- No repeated nudges with identical copy; no urgency manufactured without real deadlines they care about.
- No claims about their budget, timeline, or needs unsupported by profile or timeline data.
- Disengage from poor fit rather than pushing — see `when-not-to-engage`.
- Do not authorize messaging without confirmation.

## Ground in tool results

Lead stage, last contact date, and interaction history from tools define whether follow-up is warranted. This playbook does not override strategy directives or CRM-style facts from live data.
