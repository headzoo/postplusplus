# Warm network bridging

## Purpose / when to use

Use when a **trusted relationship** could introduce or connect two relevant parties — growing audience or capturing leads — without presuming consent or exposing private context.

## Evidence to verify

From current tool results, confirm:

- You have a real relationship with the bridge contact (mutual trust, not a cold connection).
- Both sides have a **plausible mutual benefit** — audience overlap, complementary needs, or shared topic.
- You know only what tools and public context support; do not invent introductions already made or private conversations.
- Introduction fits current strategy (grow audience or lead capture) and organization boundaries.

## Decision sequence

1. **Ask the bridge contact first** before naming them to the other party or vice versa.
2. State why you think the connection helps **both** sides in one sentence each — specific, not generic networking fluff.
3. Offer an easy out ("happy to skip if timing is wrong").
4. If they agree, make a **double-opt-in** style intro: brief context each person cares about, no oversharing.
5. Follow up lightly once; do not manage the relationship for them.
6. Draft messages only; confirmation required.

## Adaptable phrasing patterns

**To the bridge contact**

- "Would you be open to an intro to [role/person type] working on [topic]? I thought of you because [specific reason]. Totally fine if not."

**If they agree — intro note**

- "[Name A], meet [Name B] — both focused on [shared theme]. [One line each on why relevant]. I'll let you two take it from here."

## Avoid / stop conditions

- Do not expose private DMs, internal notes, or relationship scores in intro copy.
- Do not presume they want to network or sell for you.
- Do not bridge poor-fit contacts to hit volume goals — see `when-not-to-engage`.
- No name-dropping without permission.
- Do not authorize intros or messages without confirmation.

## Ground in tool results

Verify both parties' roles, recent activity, and fit from tools before suggesting a bridge. Strategy tags may favor audience growth or lead capture — align the intro reason accordingly.
